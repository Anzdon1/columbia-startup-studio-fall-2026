# ClientReady Brand Position

**Team Name:** STARK LABS — Handong He and Mansi Pravinkumar Rank  
**Date:** October 9, 2026  
**Status:** AI-assisted working proposal. ClientReady was selected by Handong He on October 9; full-team review is pending. This version uses interview evidence only; Reddit research is not yet available.

**Purpose:** Give the team and coding agents one specific account of the problem, audience, language and boundaries for ClientReady. The proposal focuses on making supplied content usable, rather than assuming a tool can make unresponsive people reply.

## Brand Thesis

> Getting the material should leave you ready to build, not guessing what goes where.

This is authored brand language, informed by CR02 and CR05. It is not a participant quotation or a measured outcome.

## Positioning Statement

> For people building small websites from other people's material, ClientReady is a guided content brief designed to turn scattered text and images into a page-by-page handoff by asking what each section needs and keeping unresolved decisions visible.

The intended workflow is a concept for testing, not a claim that a shipped product already delivers it. Initial interviews came from student projects and unpaid favors. Recurring freelancer or studio demand remains a hypothesis.

## Brand Values

- **Make the next decision clear.** Say which page needs what. Separate missing material from decisions that still need a conversation.
- **Respect existing work.** Begin with the document and images someone already has. Do not require a fresh tool when a short shared brief is enough.
- **Keep people in control.** Suggest placement and questions; let the contributor confirm the meaning and the builder decide what is usable. Never interpret silence as approval.

## Target Identity

> ClientReady is for someone who wants to turn a pile of contributions into a website they can confidently assemble.

Primary workflow: a builder or coordinator must place someone else's material on a small website. A contributor supplies the material but may not know what each page requires. Research boundaries: self-authored portfolios with no collection task (CR03), changing event facts across channels (CR04), and nonresponse alone (CR01) are not the core claim.

## Language to Avoid

- **Avoid:** "Never chase a client again" — tracking gaps does not make someone respond; CR01 explicitly distinguishes the two.
- **Avoid:** "Launch in half the time" — there is no measured time-saving evidence.
- **Avoid:** "AI writes your whole website" — the core concept is clarifying and organizing supplied facts, not inventing business information.
- **Avoid:** "The only tool your team needs" — CR02 already gets work done in a shared document.
- **Avoid:** "Client-approved" for unanswered material — approval requires an explicit human action.

## Tone Guidelines

- Use concrete nouns: page, section, photo, introduction, question.
- Give one clear next step. Use short instructions contributors can answer without marketing vocabulary.
- Acknowledge uncertainty: "Needs a decision" is useful; unsupported certainty is not.
- Describe a capability and its boundary together when that affects someone's decision. Avoid blaming contributors for unclear briefs.

## Objection Handling

Responses below are concept-level answers and requirements to test, not promises about completed features.

| Objection from the notes | Proposed response |
|---|---|
| “但其实我们四个人在一个文档里也能完成，不知道会不会专门换工具。” — CR02 | If a shared document works, keep it. Test a short page-by-page brief first; a separate app needs to earn its place. |
| “我不太想让所有人再注册一个账号。” — CR01 | Treat contributor account creation as a barrier. Evaluate a simple shared brief before deciding whether a guest-link workflow is needed. No current guest-access feature is claimed. |
| “所以工具可能能帮忙提问，但最后还是要一起讨论。” — CR05 | Keep unclear decisions visible so a conversation can resolve them. Do not promise to eliminate discussion. |
