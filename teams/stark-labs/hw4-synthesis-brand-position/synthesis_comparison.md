### Patterns — English translation of the supplied reflection

In the current interview responses, the most repeated ClientReady problems are material scattered across group chats and shared documents, incomplete contributions, text of uneven length, and uncertainty about which version is current after information changes. Each task seems small on its own, but the person responsible for the website must keep following up, organizing and confirming information.

For Strata, the main problems are checking extraction accuracy: misaligned tables, recognition errors in scans, and the need to check years and units.

### Surprises — English translation

The biggest surprise is that website content may be delayed because people do not know what to provide, rather than because they are unwilling to help. A request such as “write a project introduction” sounds simple, but the contributor may not know how long it should be, what information to include, or which images to supply. Their contribution may still need repeated revision.

That suggests ClientReady's potential value is more than storing files in one place. It could turn vague requests into small, answerable questions so contributors can finish their part more easily.

### Overall interpretation — English translation

I currently prefer ClientReady. It addresses a concrete process: collect text, photos and information from other people, then organize it into material that can be used on website pages. For students managing organization sites, event pages, or websites for other people, the work may be simple but fragmented and capable of slowing progress.

Compared with Strata, ClientReady makes it easier to define a small initial version and test whether step-by-step questions, a material checklist, and organization by page reduce follow-up and rework. Strata needs further evidence that extraction is reliable and that checking the output does not cancel out the time saved.

I would not target all Columbia students. The priority should be students who repeatedly build or maintain websites for others and collect contributions from multiple people. The next test is whether they will use ClientReady on a real project, whether it reduces back-and-forth, and whether contributors find it convenient.

### 3. Contradictions and possible explanations

These differences are left unresolved. The explanations below are hypotheses about contexts, not claims that one participant is more correct.

#### ClientReady

- **Waiting versus working:** CR01's main delay was waiting for a missing item; CR02 and CR05 already had material but needed to reshape it. Event coordination, group authorship, and a friend-to-builder handoff may create distinct jobs. A collection checklist could help visibility without solving either nonresponse or content decisions.
- **Collection matters versus collection is easy:** CR01 experienced missing material; CR03 received the requested screenshots the same day and struggled with self-expression. Someone assembling other people's contributions and someone authoring a personal portfolio may need different products.
- **Helpful guidance versus sufficient existing tools:** CR02 sees possible value in section guidance but doubts the benefit of leaving Google Docs. CR05 sees potential relief from pre-sorted material while expecting discussion to remain necessary. Different team size, handoff complexity, and existing collaboration may explain the difference; the notes do not establish which factor dominates.
- **Initial readiness versus later correctness:** CR05 needs a usable initial handoff; CR04 needs current information after changes. Both involve website content, but success at the first job would not necessarily solve the second.

#### Strata

- **Tolerable inconvenience versus meaningful setup or review work:** ST01 handles occasional uncopyable excerpts manually; ST05 reads scans directly. ST03 must inspect unclear recognition, and ST04 had to add OCR. Extraction being optional or required by a downstream task may explain this difference. The records cannot compare task frequency precisely.
- **Extract more versus help me verify:** ST02 conditionally wants table extraction but would keep checking; ST03 favors uncertainty flags. ST04 is evaluating integration and output quality. These are different definitions of useful improvement, and the interviews do not establish a single priority across them.
- **Use decision versus permission decision:** ST04 can assess a course project's integration; ST03 cannot authorize external uploading alone. The difference may reflect ownership and institutional responsibilities. Enthusiasm from an operator may not translate into adoption approval.
- **Cost-saving thesis versus interview priorities:** No participant demonstrates paid routing-cost pressure. This is a tension between the product hypothesis and this sample, rather than proof that production teams lack the problem. The sample is too different from production budget owners to resolve it.

### 4. Surprises and exceptions

#### ClientReady

- CR05's content was described as ready before the builder received it, yet selection and page placement were unresolved. File availability and usable content are distinct states.
- CR03's difficulty was explaining personal work, even though obtaining the one outside contribution was easy. This suggests an adjacent authoring use case, not validation of the original collection concept.
- CR04 wanted a reliable current version more than page organization. A small amount of content can still cause coordination work when it changes.
- CR01 describes a multi-day delay while saying actual organization was quick. Calendar delay must not be converted into hours of labor saved.

#### Strata

- ST04 had already considered simple extraction followed by a fallback. That sequencing alone may not distinguish an outside solution for this participant.
- ST03's desired output includes visible uncertainty and requires another person's approval for external handling. More extracted text alone would miss both concerns.
- ST01 cares about retaining page numbers despite low interest in a dedicated tool. This is a traceability request, not a purchase signal.
- ST05 uses PDFs often without needing extraction. File-format exposure is a weak recruitment criterion on its own.

### 6. Research implications

**ClientReady:** The next hypothesis to test is whether people repeatedly building websites for others will adopt a lightweight way to turn received material into page-ready content. Recruit people with recent repeated handoffs; ask to inspect redacted examples of the material received and the final pages. Test section guidance and placement separately from reminders, version management, and personal writing. Measure actual preparation time and repeat use before claiming time savings or paid demand.

**Strata:** Recruit people who own recurring extraction workflows and can discuss processing bills, quality thresholds, review effort, and switching authority. Test whether cost is a problem before presenting routing as the solution. For review-heavy cases, investigate source traceability and uncertainty handling separately from cost reduction. A course-project integrator and a research assistant can inform usability, but cannot stand in for a production buyer.

**Comparative decision:** These interviews alone do not validate either original commercial proposition. ClientReady's narrower preparation problem has concrete examples in CR02 and CR05 and is a reasonable next research focus. This is an AI recommendation based on evidence fit, not a recorded team decision or proof of market demand. It must not be relabeled as the team's independent synthesis.

## Part C — AI-assisted comparison, for team review

Both the supplied team reflection and the AI analysis favor testing a narrower ClientReady concept. The team's reflection emphasizes the contributor's experience: a vague request might become easier to answer when divided into small questions. The AI does not contradict that idea, but it is more explicit about the evidentiary limits. It distinguishes CR01's waiting, CR02's uneven contributions, CR05's selection and placement decisions, and CR04's changing information. It also retains CR03 as a case with little collection pain. Those distinctions were less explicit in the team's reflection and prevent “website content problems” from becoming one oversized feature list. For Strata, the team emphasizes accuracy and checking effort; the AI additionally highlights the lack of measured processing costs and the different adoption conditions of an RA needing permission and a developer evaluating integration. The team gives more weight to the practical advantage of a small first version of ClientReady; that is a product-development judgment, not an interview finding.
