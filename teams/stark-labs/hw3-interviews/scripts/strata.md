# Strata interview guide

This is a proposed structured guide prepared when organizing the interview materials. Exact original questions were not retained in the supplied notes, so this document is not presented as the verbatim script used in the completed conversations.

Target: people with direct responsibility for document extraction, OCR, integration, evaluation or review. Distinguish task users from pipeline owners and budget decision-makers.

## Opening (about 2 minutes)

Hi, we're students working on a class project at Columbia. We'd like to understand your own experience with this workflow. This should take about 20–25 minutes. Honest feedback, including that something is not a problem for you, is useful. Is it okay if we take notes?

If recording is desired, ask for permission separately before starting.

## Background (2–3 minutes)

1. What document-processing work have you done, and what were you personally responsible for?
2. What documents and approximate volumes did you handle? Was this a one-off assignment or an ongoing workflow?

## Problem exploration (5–7 minutes)

3. Walk me through a recent file or batch, from receiving it to using its contents.
4. What was hardest, if anything? Tell me about the last specific incident.
5. Why was that difficult? What did you do next?
6. What time, rework or other consequences did it cause? How often does that happen?

## Current solutions (3–4 minutes)

7. What tools do you use? How do you decide which method to apply?
8. How do you check the results? Which errors matter most?
9. What have you tried to improve this? What happened?
10. Have you or your organization paid for this workflow? What costs do you know and which are unknown?

## Solution signal (3–4 minutes; introduce the idea only now)

We're exploring Strata. It would try simple extraction where appropriate, use OCR or more capable models when needed, and record routes and costs. We want to test whether that could reduce total cost while meeting a team's quality requirements.
11. Have you tried an approach like that before?
12. What's your initial reaction?
13. What would it help with in your example, and what would it leave unresolved?
14. What concerns or requirements would affect a trial? Who would approve it?

## Wrap-up (about 2 minutes)

15. If you could change one thing about this workflow, what would it be?
16. What should I have asked but didn't?
17. Could you introduce us to someone else with relevant experience?
18. Would you be open to a ten-minute follow-up in a couple of weeks?

## Interviewer reminders

Listen more than you speak. Ask for recent examples rather than hypothetical purchases. Do not imply that free tools have zero total operating cost. Record exact words where possible, and separate observation from interpretation. A referral suggestion is not a completed introduction; interest in a trial is not a scheduled trial or a purchase.
