# Findings across the ten interviews
This analysis compares ten team-confirmed interview accounts. Signal colors are interpretations of the notes, not participant scores. No Green rating is assigned because these accounts do not establish strong validation of the original business hypotheses.

## ClientReady
The five accounts describe different problems. CR01 waited for a missing biography. CR02 received uneven contributions and edited them together. CR03 mainly struggled to explain personal projects. CR04 needed consistent updates after event details changed. CR05 received a long document and images that still needed selection and page mapping.

CR01, CR02 and CR05 all involve content coordination broadly, but they should not be counted as three people describing the same specific pain in the same way. The clearest candidate overlap is CR02 and CR05: material exists, but needs guidance or organization before it is ready for a page. That is two of five accounts, with different project contexts. The supplied evidence does not clearly satisfy the course's 3-of-5 rule for the original missing-client-content hypothesis.

No account establishes payment for a dedicated content-collection solution. CR01, CR02 and CR04 explicitly describe no relevant spending. CR03 distinguishes website spending from collection spending. CR05 reports no dedicated tools and unpaid website work, but does not explicitly quantify spending on solving the collection problem. The evening in CR02 includes other website work; the extra hours in CR05 are a rough estimate. These should not be reported as measured collection costs.

Strongest relevant excerpt, translated from CR05:
“I had to work out what belonged on the homepage and what we didn't need.”

Provisional decision: narrow the next research round to people who repeatedly turn other people's material into website pages. Interview actual freelancers or small studios, as well as contributors, to separate organization problems from nonresponse and unclear messaging. Test whether a short guided content brief improves a real handoff before assuming a separate tool or subscription is warranted.

## Strata
ST01 and ST05 mainly read PDFs and do not describe a strong extraction need. ST02 reports table transfer and data-comparability problems. ST03 reports manual review of blurry scans and unknown external-processing permissions. ST04 reports an integration problem when scans produced empty output, followed by adding OCR.

ST02, ST03 and ST04 describe three different technical or review difficulties. Grouping all three as 'PDF problems' would be too broad to establish three matching accounts. None of the five establishes painful paid-model spending or quantifies a cost-saving opportunity. ST03 does not know whether the institution pays for its software; that must remain unknown. ST04's free tools and small tests do not establish zero infrastructure or engineering cost.

Strongest relevant excerpt, translated from ST04:
“Then we tried scanned files and found that the extracted output was empty.”

A separate concern, translated from ST02:
“I'm more concerned about getting something that looks correct but is actually wrong than saving a little money.”

Provisional decision: narrow the next research round to engineers who maintain recurring mixed-document workflows and can discuss volume, extraction quality, review effort, costs and tool selection. ST04 is the closest technical match in these accounts; ST03 can inform review needs but does not own the entire pipeline. Treat low-friction integration and visible uncertainty as adjacent hypotheses to investigate, not validated features demanded by all five people.
