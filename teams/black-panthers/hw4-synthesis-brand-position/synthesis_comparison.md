## **PART C: Comparison ( [Jaden Hinton](mailto:jh4792@columbia.edu))**

The two syntheses agree on the core finding: discovery itself is not the main problem; the friction around it is. Both identify planning effort, saved posts that never get revisited, travel distance, cost, and group plans that fall apart as the recurring pain points, and your overall read (“everything around discovery... on a silver platter”) matches my patterns on planning, navigation, and timing. The main difference is structure. Your synthesis is organized by concept and reads as a case for Idea 1, while mine runs themes across all 12 interviews and spends more time on where people disagreed: crowds as a draw for scene-chasers like Jaden Barret and Yabby versus a deterrent for Genna and Sarika, group versus solo, and more ratings versus taste-matched ones. Those tensions, and the segments they suggest, don’t appear in your version, and they matter if you plan to merge both ideas into one map. Your synthesis also adds things mine couldn’t, such as the specific framing of proximity as a user-configurable setting and the budget-bounded day plan, which come from interviewer judgment rather than the notes alone.

The biggest difference between the two comes down to source material. Your synthesis draws on the raw notes, which is why it has verbatim quotes (“I have so many places saved and I never go to any of them,” “If it’s more than like 30 minutes away, I’m not going”) and a concrete distance threshold, while I was working from summarized notes that contained only one clearly verbatim line. Your quotes are stronger evidence than anything I could pull, and they land squarely on the Idea 1 pain points. Scoping “discovery is a non-issue” to Idea 1 also holds up against the notes: none of the Idea 1 interviewees described struggling to find things they were interested in. The one nuance worth carrying forward is that Joshua, Jaden Barret, and Kishanti each said their preferred category (gaming spots, fashion pop-ups, community events) is poorly organized on existing maps. That is less a discovery problem than a findability problem, and it fits naturally into planning: a day can’t be built around a place the map can’t categorize. With Idea 2 scrapped, the crowd-avoidance findings mostly fall away, but the scene-chasers’ desire for real-time buzz is still an Idea 1 need and may be worth keeping in the planning flow.
