# Persona Reactions to the Plandit Brand Position

Each persona was shown the final `brand_position.md` and asked what was clear, what would make
them hesitate, and whether the position represented their needs. Different reactions are
intentional: the personas represent different constraints and should not all approve for the same
reason.

## Sofia Ramirez — the saver who never goes

**Verdict:** Approve (9/10)

Sofia recognized herself immediately in "save places they want to go but never actually make it
there." The promise to start with her existing interests, keep the plan nearby, and make it
ready-to-go addresses her main frustration. Her strongest reaction was that the position avoids
offering another discovery feed.

**Reaction:** "This is literally my saved folder problem. If you can turn the places I already
want into a day I can follow, that's what I need."

## Amara Okafor — the group-chat planner

**Verdict:** Approve with conditions (6/10)

Amara liked the emphasis on turning vague intent into a specific, shareable plan and the canonical
language around "with your friends" and "the group." Her condition is that group sharing must be
visible in the product, not only in the internal brand document. Friends should be able to open a
plan without downloading the app or creating accounts.

**Reaction:** "Good—make the plan for me. Just don't give me another thing I have to convince
everyone in the group chat to download."

## Grace Lin — the budget-stretcher

**Verdict:** Approve (8/10)

Grace responded to "within your budget," "worth it," and the decision to treat cost and distance
as core planning inputs. She would expect the product to show real estimated totals, including
transportation, rather than vague price symbols.

**Reaction:** "On budget and nearby is the right promise. Show me what the whole day costs before
I agree to it."

## Marcus Bell — the student-athlete with a short free window

**Verdict:** Approve with conditions (5/10)

Marcus liked the practical language, proximity promise, and focus on getting to a usable plan
quickly. The saved-interests framing does not fully represent him because he rarely saves places.
Plandit needs a quick path for users who start with only a time window, distance, budget, and a few
interests.

**Reaction:** "The nearby plan part works. I just don't have a big saved list, so let me tell you
I have three hours and show me something that's open."

## Andre Wallace — the scene chaser

**Verdict:** Reject as a primary-user position (4/10)

Andre found the position too focused on old saves and not focused enough on what is current today.
That is expected: he is a secondary stress-test persona, while the final brand position
deliberately rejects discovery as the main promise. His reaction is useful for any future live-map
or time-sensitive feature, but it should not pull the core brand back toward a discovery feed.

**Reaction:** "This helps me plan places I already know. It doesn't tell me whether they're still
the places people care about tonight."

## Changes resulting from the test

- Kept the core position centered on saved intent, planning, budget, and proximity.
- Kept group and solo language so the product is not framed as group-only.
- Preserved a path for users without saved places in the product guardrails and persona tests.
- Did not add real-time discovery to the brand thesis; Andre remains a secondary persona for
  evaluating future live or time-sensitive features.
