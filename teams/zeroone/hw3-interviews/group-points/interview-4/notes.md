* ## **Problem interview**

* Yuval Gerber: How does your group make predictions?  
* Daniel: With college friends, usually in person or over iMessage.  
* Yuval Gerber: Describe a recent disagreement.  
* Daniel: Two of us predicted which team would finish higher and assigned twenty local tokens to the winner. Weeks later, one of us thought the agreement referred to the playoffs instead.  
* Yuval Gerber: What record could you check?  
* Daniel: Nothing from the original conversation. A later text named the teams but did not say what counted as winning.  
* Yuval Gerber: How did it end?  
* Daniel: We left it unscored rather than argue. We both remembered the agreement, but remembered different versions.  
* Yuval Gerber: What do you use now?  
* Daniel: Messages, occasional screenshots, and a phone note for token totals. I have not paid for a dedicated tool.

* ## **Concept reaction**

* Interviewer: A shared space for friend-group predictions and challenges using only local points or tokens. Members confirm rules and participants; a manager records results; scoring and group standings update automatically.  
* Yuval Gerber: What would have helped in that example?  
* Daniel: A short sentence that both sides confirm before the prediction starts. That is useful for agreements that stay open for weeks.  
* Yuval Gerber: What control would you want over the rules?  
* Daniel: The confirmed terms should be locked. A manager should not be able to silently change the terms or result.  
* Yuval Gerber: Would you record every prediction?  
* Daniel: No. Quick jokes can stay in the chat. For longer ones, confirmation needs to take seconds.
