* ## **Problem interview**

* Zhangcheng Kang: What does your friend group usually do together?  
* Liam: We watch NBA games online and talk in a Discord voice channel. We also use the text chat for reactions, memes, and predictions.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: Tell me about a recent prediction you made together.  
* Liam: During the playoffs we predicted which teams would win their series and make the Finals. It was mainly for fun and bragging rights. We agreed in the call rather than setting detailed rules.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: How did you check the predictions afterward?  
* Liam: We searched or scrolled back through Discord. Sometimes someone took a screenshot, but there were so many messages that finding an old pick could take a while.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: What happened when nobody remembered?  
* Liam: Usually we laughed about it or forgot to keep score. Nobody was particularly upset. Discord is good enough most of the time.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: Have you paid for a tool to handle this?  
* Liam: No. I have never paid for something to track our predictions.

* ## **Concept reaction**

* Interviewer: A shared space for friend-group predictions and challenges using only local points or tokens. Members confirm rules and participants; a manager records results; scoring and group standings update automatically.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: What would a shared record with local points have changed?  
* Liam: For a larger group making lots of playoff predictions, it could save searching through messages. For a simple prediction between two people, I would probably just text.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: What would make you hesitate?  
* Liam: Another app might be more effort than the problem deserves. If the manager also participates, I would not want that person to be the only one deciding the result.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: What matters most to your group?  
* Liam: The conversation and bragging rights. Keeping a precise score matters only when there are lots of predictions over multiple rounds.
