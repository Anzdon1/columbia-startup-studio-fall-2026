## **Problem interview**

Elad Tisch: Where do your group agreements happen?

Noah: Mostly in person or in our college group chat. We go out, watch sports, and occasionally make predictions.

Elad Tisch: Walk me through the Knicks example.

Noah: Someone thought the Knicks could win the championship, someone disagreed, and we made an informal agreement. We never wrote down all the details.

Elad Tisch: What specifically went wrong?

Noah: Later we remembered the prediction but not one of the terms. There was no written record of the participants or the exact agreement.

Elad Tisch: Was the result itself difficult to decide?

Noah: No. Whether the Knicks won was objective. Remembering what we agreed to was the difficult part. It was awkward, but not a major conflict.

Elad Tisch: What do you do now?

Noah: We rely on memory or messages in the group chat. We do not have a dedicated shared record.

## **Concept reaction**

Interviewer: A shared space for friend-group predictions and challenges using only local points or tokens. Members confirm rules and participants; a manager records results; scoring and group standings update automatically.

Elad Tisch: How would a shared local-points record have helped?

Noah: Having everyone confirm the same terms when we made the prediction would have helped more than anything afterward.

Elad Tisch: What would stop you from using it?

Noah: Too many steps. These conversations are spontaneous, so recording an agreement would have to take seconds.

Elad Tisch: Who should decide the result?

Noah: For an obvious sports outcome, a manager could record it. For something subjective, everyone should be able to agree.
