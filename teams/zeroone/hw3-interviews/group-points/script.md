How friends make and follow through on group agreements

This document contains two interview scripts, eleven interviews conducted by our team, and eleven corresponding snapshots. Group challenges use local points or tokens to keep score and display group standings.

Interviewer guide Allow 20 to 25 minutes. Ask for specific past experiences. Introduce the product only in Part 5\. Follow interesting answers instead of reading every prompt verbatim.

## **1 Opening 2 minutes**

“Hi. I’m working on a class project about how groups of friends make informal plans and agreements, and what happens when people need to follow through later. I’m trying to understand real experiences, not pitch an app. Would you mind talking for about 20 minutes?”

“Please be honest. If something has never been a problem for you, that’s useful for me to know too.”

## **2 Their group 2 to 3 minutes**

What groups of friends are you part of? What do you organize or decide together?

Where do those conversations usually happen?

Tell me about a recent informal agreement your group made.

If they mention a prediction, challenge, or bet, follow that example. Otherwise ask about the most recent playful prediction or challenge. If none comes to mind, do not force the topic.

## **3 One real example 5 to 7 minutes**

Walk me through what happened, from the first conversation to the end.

How did everyone agree on the rules? Did you keep score or record who won?

How did you know who was participating? Did anyone write the details down?

How was the result decided, and what happened afterward?

Did anyone forget, disagree, or need a reminder? What did you do?

If they organized it, ask what work fell on them. If they participated, ask what they relied on the organizer to do. Return to details: “What happened next?” and “Can you show me how you kept track?”

## **4 Current habits 3 to 4 minutes**

How often does something like this happen in your group?

What tools or habits do you use now? Have you tried anything else?

What works well? What gets awkward or forgotten?

Have you spent time checking old predictions, reminding someone, or resolving a disagreement about the score? Tell me about the last time.

If the example caused no real difficulty, ask about adjacent group agreements and record that the original problem may be weak.

## **5 Brief concept 3 to 4 minutes**

“We’re exploring a shared place for friend groups to make friendly predictions and challenges using only local points or tokens. A group manager could invite members, set the question and point rules, and record the result. The app would keep score, update group standings, and let everyone see their prediction history.”

What is your first reaction? Which part would have helped in your last example?

Which part seems unnecessary or would make you hesitate?

How would your group feel about one manager deciding the result?

Would automatic scorekeeping and group standings make this more fun, or feel too serious? Why?

What do you use now that comes closest to this?

Listen without defending the idea or asking whether they would use it.

## **6 Wrap 2 minutes**

What did I miss about how your group handles this?

Who else regularly organizes these kinds of things and might be willing to talk?

May I follow up if I have one or two more questions?

Thank them for being candid.

## **After the interview**

Within one hour, record a short snapshot with: participant and group role; a specific past example; problem mentioned before the concept; exact quote showing pain or lack of it; current workaround and time or money involved; reaction and concern; referrals or follow-up; and anything that surprised you.
