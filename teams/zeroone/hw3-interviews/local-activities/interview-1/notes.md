* ## **Problem interview**

* Zhangcheng Kang: What was your social circle like when you moved to New York?  
* Ryan: I came here for graduate school and did not know many people. Most people I knew were classmates I was not close to yet.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: Tell me about a time that affected a plan.  
* Ryan: There was a school boat cruise. I wanted to go, but I did not want to take the train alone and then stand at the event without anyone I knew.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: How did you try to find company?  
* Ryan: I asked a few classmates. Some were busy and some already had plans. I thought I might skip it if nobody joined.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: What happened?  
* Ryan: One classmate was interested, so we took the train together. Once I knew someone was coming, I felt much better.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: What do you use now, and have you paid for help?  
* Ryan: I ask classmates or check WeChat and school chats. I have not paid for a companion-finding service. If everyone is busy, sometimes I do not go.

* ## **Concept reaction**

* Interviewer: A platform where people nearby post a specific activity and time so interested, available people can join.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: How would activity matching have helped?  
* Ryan: For the boat cruise, I would have checked whether another student wanted to go together. It would have been more useful when I first arrived than now.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: What would make a match comfortable?  
* Ryan: Knowing who the person is, preferably another student. A small group feels easier than meeting one stranger alone.  
* Zhangcheng Kang: What is the main problem?  
* Ryan: Having someone to go with, rather than finding the event itself.
