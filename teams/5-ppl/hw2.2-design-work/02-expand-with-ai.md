# Exercise 2: Expand with AI

We picked three flips from Exercise 1 and ran each through the class prompt with an AI model. Each run produced 10 problem/solution concepts. The 1-10 scores are the model's estimates, not research findings.

## Prompt

```text
Act as a product designer doing divergent thinking exercises.
Take this problem and starting idea and generate business ideas.
[PROBLEM]
[STARTING IDEA]
Generate 10 diverse problem-solution pairs. For each:
1. The specific problem
2. Why this persona cares
3. A simple solution concept
4. Likelihood this is a real, painful problem (1-10)
```

## A: A reminder you can't ignore, shared with friends (from Idea 5)

**Problem:** Students forget to drink enough water on busy days, and reminder apps are easy to ignore.
**Starting idea:** Turn hydration into a small group challenge with friends, with a nudge that is hard to ignore.

| ID | Concept | Specific problem | Why this persona cares | Simple solution | Likelihood real and painful (1-10) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | SipSquad | Students forget to drink water on long class days, and solo reminders get dismissed. | A student who feels drained by late afternoon wants an easy habit that sticks. | Three to five friends form a group. Each person logs a glass with one tap, and the group sees a shared daily streak. | 6 |
| A2 | NudgeBuddy | App notifications are easy to swipe away. | Someone who has tried reminder apps and quit wants a reminder they will act on. | When you fall behind, a friend in your group gets a prompt to text you. | 5 |
| A3 | RefillMap | Students do not know where the working refill stations are, so they buy bottled drinks. | A student wants to save money and not carry extra water. | A campus map of refill stations that students add to and rate. | 5 |
| A4 | HabitCircle | Habit apps track one person, and most people quit within a week. | A student wants accountability without a coach or a paid program. | A small-group challenge for any daily habit (water, steps, bedtime) with a weekly check-in. | 6 |
| A5 | OneTap | Logging water by hand is tedious, so people stop. | A student who already carries a bottle wants tracking with no typing. | A home-screen web page with one big button for their bottle size, so logging takes a second. | 4 |
| A6 | CaffeineSwap | During exams, students replace water with coffee and energy drinks. | A student wants to feel less jittery and spend less. | A two-week challenge to swap one caffeinated drink a day for water, showing money saved. | 5 |
| A7 | HotDayPlan | On hot days, people walking around the city underestimate how much water they need. | A commuter wants to avoid a miserable afternoon. | A daily goal based on the forecast, with reminders timed to when you will be outside. | 4 |
| A8 | FloorChallenge | Dorm floors have little that gets residents interacting. | An RA wants a low-effort floor activity during finals. | A floor-wide water leaderboard with a small prize at the end of the week. | 3 |
| A9 | FlavorClub | Plain water is boring, so people reach for soda. | A student wants something tastier without sugar. | Friends share and rate simple infusion ideas, with a weekly theme. | 3 |
| A10 | LoserBuysCoffee | Challenges without stakes fizzle out. | Friends want a reason to keep going. | The group agrees on a stake, like the last-place person buys coffee. The app tracks it but handles no money. | 4 |

## B: Help latecomers catch up without interrupting (from Idea 4)

**Problem:** People arrive late to or miss meetings, then interrupt to ask what they missed or never learn their tasks.
**Starting idea:** Automatically send whoever was late or absent a short summary and their action items.

| ID | Concept | Specific problem | Why this persona cares | Simple solution | Likelihood real and painful (1-10) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | CatchUp | A group-project member who arrives late interrupts to ask what they missed. | The late student wants to catch up quietly, and the others do not want to repeat themselves. | A live notes link that shows a three-line summary of what has happened so far. | 6 |
| B2 | MissedIt | Members who skip a meeting never learn their action items. | A project lead wants tasks to get done without chasing people. | After the meeting, each absent member automatically gets the decisions and their own tasks. | 7 |
| B3 | StartOnTime | Meetings start late because everyone waits for the last person. | On-time members lose ten minutes every meeting. | A shared timer with a start-without-them rule and an automatic recap for late arrivals. | 5 |
| B4 | WalkTime | Students underestimate the walk between classes and meetings. | A student who is always five minutes late wants a better warning. | Reminders based on walking time between campus buildings, pulled from their calendar. | 6 |
| B5 | ClubRecap | Club members who miss a weekly meeting gradually drop out. | A club leader wants to keep members involved. | A one-page weekly recap built from the meeting notes and posted to the group chat. | 5 |
| B6 | AgendaBox | Meetings without an agenda run long and make people late for the next one. | Students with back-to-back commitments want meetings that end on time. | A simple time-boxed agenda template shared before every meeting. | 5 |
| B7 | AsyncStandup | Finding a time when a whole group can meet is hard, so meetings get skipped. | A team with clashing schedules still needs to stay in sync. | Each member answers three short questions by a deadline instead of meeting live. | 6 |
| B8 | StudyGroupRSVP | Study groups fall apart when people show up late or not at all. | A student organizing a group wants to know who is coming. | RSVPs, reminders, and an attendance record the whole group can see. | 5 |
| B9 | ListenIn | Late arrivals cannot enter a room mid-meeting without disrupting it. | A latecomer wants to follow along on the way in. | A one-tap audio link to listen in, then join at the next break. | 4 |
| B10 | FriendETA | Friends are chronically late to dinners and plans. | The person who is always first wants to stop waiting alone. | Everyone shares an ETA before a plan, and the group sees an on-time record. | 4 |

## C: Test prep that fits into your day (from Idea 6)

**Problem:** Adults preparing for standardized tests (GRE, GMAT, LSAT, and the SAT for test takers 18 and over) struggle to practice every day, review their mistakes, and stay motivated alone.
**Starting idea:** Short daily question sets, automatic review of wrong answers, and studying with friends.

| ID | Concept | Specific problem | Why this persona cares | Simple solution | Likelihood real and painful (1-10) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | DailyFive | Test takers plan long study sessions, skip them, and fall behind. | A working professional studying for the GMAT has little free time. | Five questions a day at a time you choose, with a streak. It takes under ten minutes. | 7 |
| C2 | MistakeLoop | People keep doing new questions but never go back to the ones they got wrong. | A GRE taker keeps making the same mistakes. | Every wrong answer comes back on a spaced schedule until you get it right twice. | 7 |
| C3 | StudyDuo | Studying alone for months is lonely and easy to quit. | An LSAT taker wants someone to keep them on track. | Pair up with a friend taking any test. You see each other's daily progress, not scores. | 6 |
| C4 | UnlockDrill | People pick up their phone to scroll instead of studying. | Someone wants idle phone time to turn into practice. | An opt-in shortcut that shows one question when you open an app you chose. | 5 |
| C5 | CrossTrain | Verbal and math skills overlap across tests, but prep materials are split by test. | A student deciding between the GRE and GMAT wants to prepare for both. | Questions tagged by skill, so practice counts toward more than one test. | 5 |
| C6 | PaceCheck | Test takers rush and make careless errors under time pressure. | Someone whose scores drop on timed sections wants to know why. | Tracks time per question and shows where rushing leads to mistakes. | 6 |
| C7 | ErrorJournal | People do not know why they got a question wrong. | A test taker wants to fix the cause, not just see the right answer. | After each wrong answer, pick a reason (careless, concept, time). A weekly summary shows the patterns. | 6 |
| C8 | StudyRoom | Friends preparing for tests at the same time do not study together. | A student focuses better when friends are working too. | A shared online room with a timer where friends join a focused session. | 5 |
| C9 | PlanFromDate | First-time test takers do not know how to plan from now until test day. | A student with a test date in three months wants a clear plan. | Enter your test date and weekly hours to get a week-by-week plan that adjusts when you miss a day. | 6 |
| C10 | PeerExplain | Explanations in prep books can be hard to follow. | A test taker stuck on a hard question wants a clearer explanation. | Users post short explanations for hard questions, and the clearest ones rise to the top. | 5 |
