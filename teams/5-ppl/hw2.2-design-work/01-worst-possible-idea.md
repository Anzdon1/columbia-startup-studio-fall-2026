# Exercise 1: Worst possible idea

Each person started one terrible solution to a real problem. Everyone else made it worse with "Yes, and it also…". Then we flipped each one: what is interesting or good in it? We worked in a shared Google Doc. Entries below are copied from it as written.

## Round 1

### Idea 1: A hat that floats over your head

**Started by:** Arjun Joshi
**Real problem:** Hats always blow off in the wind.

| Teammate | "Yes, and it also…" |
| --- | --- |
| Linfeng Li | The hat has holes in it. |
| Yicheng Ying | It floats three feet above your head. |
| Cody Wang | Battery lasts only 5 mins |
| Arjun Joshi | Cool Party Trick |
| Kushaan Vardhan | Made of materials that rips easily |

**Flip:**

- The low battery life means you do not have to use it for long.
- It could be a cool party trick.
- Putting a camera on top could be cool.
- You get to feel the rain and enjoy it.

### Idea 2: A triangle bedframe and mattress

**Started by:** Linfeng Li
**Real problem:** It could fit into a room with limited space.

| Teammate | "Yes, and it also…" |
| --- | --- |
| Linfeng Li | (started it) Yes, and it also is an equilateral triangle. |
| Yicheng Ying | Triangle blankes only |
| Cody Wang | Sold in sets of 6 |
| Arjun Joshi | It rotates during your sleep |
| Kushaan Vardhan | Pointy end is pillow end |

**Flip:**

- A triangle really does fit into corners and awkward spaces where a rectangle can't.
- Sold in sets of six means it's modular, so you can build a hexagon, a row, or a bench, and the bed adapts to different rooms.
- The rotating base points to a real idea: beds that move or fold away to free up floor space during the day.
- Triangle blankets are a reminder that bedding is designed for beds, not rooms.

### Idea 3: GPS backpacks for pigeons

**Started by:** Yicheng (Eason) Ying
**Real problem:** Cities have too many pigeons.
**Bad idea:** Give every pigeon a small backpack with a GPS tracker and a tiny speaker that plays a recorded message asking it to relocate to the suburbs.

| Teammate | "Yes, and it also…" |
| --- | --- |
| Linfeng Li | Heavy backpacks |
| Yicheng Ying | Speakers only play at 3am |
| Cody Wang | The message is in latin |
| Arjun Joshi | A rival program for seagulls launches this week to be directed into the city that steals the backpacks |
| Kushaan Vardhan | Pigeons must scan qr code to register for program |

**Flip:**

- GPS trackers on birds are real science and could reveal where city pigeons go and why they stay.
- Small sensors on animals could monitor air quality across a city.
- The 3 a.m. speaker hints at the real solution: changing the environment (sound, lighting, food access) instead of chasing the birds.
- The seagull "rival program" is ecosystem management, which is a risky tool.
- The QR-code registration is a reminder that any plan needs to be one people can run and measure.

### Idea 4: Meeting rooms that seal shut

**Started by:** Cody Wang
**Real problem:** People are always late to meetings.
**Bad idea:** Every meeting room has a slow-closing hydraulic door that seals shut at the start of the meeting and cannot be reopened until it ends. Latecomers stay outside listening to a speaker that plays the meeting audio.

| Teammate | "Yes, and it also…" |
| --- | --- |
| Linfeng Li | Door locks people in |
| Yicheng Ying | Clock is set by whoever booked the room |
| Cody Wang | Bathrooms inside the meeting rooms so no one outside can use them |
| Arjun Joshi | The rooms are booked automatically for catch-up meetings for the people who are locked out that start when the original one ends |
| Kushaan Vardhan | Hallway audio delays by 40 mins |

**Flip:**

- A hard start time with real consequences does fix lateness; a softer version (locked video calls after 5 minutes) already exists.
- The hallway audio feed means latecomers can catch up without interrupting.
- Locking people in would force meetings to end on time.
- Automatic catch-up meetings are close to a good idea: automatically sending a summary and action items to whoever missed it.
- Letting the booker set the clock suggests the meeting owner should control the schedule, not the room.

### Idea 5: A water bottle that fills your shoes

**Started by:** Kushaan Vardhan
**Real problem:** Nobody drinks enough water.
**Bad idea:** A smart water bottle that, if you fall behind on your daily intake, slowly fills your shoes with water until you catch up.

| Teammate | "Yes, and it also…" |
| --- | --- |
| Linfeng Li | Tracks by weight not sips so if you pour water out it thinks you drank it and if u refill it it thinks you're behind |
| Yicheng Ying | It syncs with your friend's, if any of your friends are dehydrated everyone's shoes gets wet |
| Cody Wang | Sparkling water only |
| Arjun Joshi | Silent mode fills your bag instead |
| Kushaan Vardhan | Cancel subscription plan and the bottle treats that as zero water consumed for rest of your life |

**Flip:**

- Wet shoes are a strong nudge; the real idea is a reminder you can't ignore.
- Syncing with friends turns hydration into a group challenge, which works for habits.
- Tracking by weight is how real smart bottles work; it just needs to be accurate.
- Silent mode shows the product could adapt to context, such as meetings or sleep.

## Round 2

After the first round, the team wanted to explore test prep, so we ran one more idea through the same exercise.

### Idea 6: Answer a test question to unlock your phone

**Started by:** Linfeng Li
**Real problem:** Preparing for standardized tests (SAT, GRE, GMAT, LSAT and others) takes steady daily practice, and it is hard to keep up.
**Bad idea:** Your phone stays locked until you answer a practice test question.

| Teammate | "Yes, and it also…" |
| --- | --- |
| Linfeng Li | (started it) |
| Yicheng Ying | It gives you questions for a test you are not studying for, like GMAT questions when you are preparing for the SAT. |
| Cody Wang | It asks a random number of questions each time. |
| Arjun Joshi | For every question you get wrong, it adds two more. |
| Kushaan Vardhan | It shares your results with your friends. |

**Flip:**

- Questions from other tests can build knowledge beyond the one test you are studying for.
- Not knowing how many questions are coming makes users slow down and answer each one carefully.
- Adding questions after a mistake points to a real feature: automatically scheduling review of the questions you got wrong.
- Sharing results with friends turns test prep into something you do together.
