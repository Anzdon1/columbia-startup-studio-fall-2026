# Our top two ideas

These are the two ideas we will interview people about next week. Both target adult test takers (GRE, GMAT, LSAT, and SAT takers 18 and over).

## 1. C4: UnlockDrill

People reach for their phone to scroll when they meant to study. UnlockDrill turns that moment into one practice question: when you open an app you chose, you answer a question first, with the option to skip. It comes from Linfeng's Round 2 bad idea, a phone that stays locked until you answer a test question.

**Who has the problem:** adults preparing for the GRE, GMAT, LSAT or SAT, especially students and working professionals who study in short gaps between other commitments.

**Why they would care:** a test date is fixed and their score affects admissions. Every study session lost to scrolling pushes them further behind, and they know it.

**What we will ask 10 test takers:** How often do they pick up their phone while studying? Have they tried app blockers or screen-time limits, and what happened? Would they turn on something that adds a question before their most-used app?

## 2. C7: ErrorJournal

Test takers see the right answer but not why they got it wrong. ErrorJournal asks for a quick reason after each mistake (careless, concept, time) and shows the patterns every week. It builds on the flip of Arjun's "Yes, and" in Idea 6: adding questions after a mistake points to reviewing mistakes on purpose.

**Who has the problem:** adult test takers who already do practice sets but whose scores have stopped improving.

**Why they would care:** practice time is limited, and repeating the same kind of mistake wastes it. Knowing which kind of mistake costs them the most points tells them what to study next.

**What we will ask 10 test takers:** What do they do after getting a practice question wrong? Do they keep track of their mistakes today? Which kind of mistake costs them the most points?
