# Worst Possible Idea

## 1. Phone Distraction While Studying

**Problem:** Students keep getting distracted by their phones while studying.

**Worst Possible Idea:** Lock every student's phone in a box for the entire semester.

**Flip:** Give students an optional phone lockbox or "focus mode" that they can use for a short period of time when they want to concentrate.

## 2. Students Being Late to Class

**Problem:** College students are always late to class.

**Worst Possible Idea:** Lock the classroom door exactly when class starts.

**Yes, and it also...**
- Charges students $50 every time they are late.
- Sends a message to the whole class saying who was late.

**Flip:** Instead of punishing students after they are late, give students reminders about when they need to leave and how long it will take to reach their classroom.

---

## 3. Students Procrastinating on Assignments

**Problem:** Students procrastinate on assignments.

**Worst Possible Idea:** Automatically delete the assignment if a student does not start it three days before the deadline.

**Flip:** Give students early reminders and break the assignment into smaller milestones.

# Lean Canvases

These canvases use the nine boxes from the class template. Each concept is a possible part of one bathroom finder platform, rather than an assumption that ten separate businesses will earn ten separate subscriptions.

**Initial market:** Test the platform with students on one university campus. Keep basic restroom search and facility information free for users.

**Future market:** Expand to shopping malls if the campus pilot demonstrates useful, accurate information and manageable maintenance costs. Shoppers and mall employees would be users; mall operators and property managers would be potential paying customers. Airports, stadiums, museums, and other large venues are later possibilities, not confirmed customers.

**Proposed revenue model:** Test venue licensing and optional setup fees first. Later options include paid facilities dashboards, white-label versions under a venue’s own brand, and clearly labeled advertisements from nearby businesses. Expanding to a mall is a customer expansion; charging the mall for the service is the revenue stream. These options could be bundled, and should not be counted as separate revenue when included in the same contract.

**Advertising and trust:** Sponsored business cards should be separate from restroom results. Advertisers cannot buy a better cleanliness score, an accessibility label, verification, or a route recommendation. Basic corrections and factual listing information should not require payment. Advertising should not use disability-related searches or private restroom preferences for targeting.

**Validation status:** Revenue, willingness to pay, partnerships, and the listed potential unfair advantages are hypotheses—not established results. A location database or an app feature alone is not an uncopyable advantage. Advertising income depends on audience size and advertiser demand; venue fees depend on a willing budget owner and a service that is affordable to maintain. Additional assumptions are flagged below each canvas.

## Lean Canvas 1 — Find the Nearest Restroom

### Problem
1. Students do not know where the nearest restroom is in unfamiliar campus buildings.
2. General map apps usually show buildings rather than exact restroom locations.
3. Searching hallways or asking other people wastes time when the need is urgent.

### Solution
1. Location-based map showing nearby campus restrooms.
2. Distance and estimated walking time to each restroom.
3. Simple directions from the user's current location.

### Unique Value Proposition
Find the nearest campus restroom in seconds without searching through an unfamiliar building.

### Unfair Advantage
A campus-specific database with exact restroom locations and building-level details that general map applications usually do not have.

### Customer Segments
- **Initial users:** First-year, transfer, and visiting students searching for restrooms in unfamiliar campus buildings.
- **Initial paying customer to test:** University student affairs or facilities departments.
- **Future users and buyers:** Shopping mall visitors as users; mall operators and property managers as paying customers.
- **Potential advertisers:** Nearby cafés, convenience stores, and mall retailers.

### Key Metrics
- Weekly restroom searches
- Repeat users
- Percentage of searches that lead to navigation
- Successful route completions
- Number of paying venue pilots and renewals; advertiser interest relative to actual local app usage.

### Channels
- Student orientation, campus communities, and approved QR codes in libraries and student centers.
- Pilot proposals to university facilities teams and mall property managers.
- Mall websites, entrance directories, and venue-branded QR codes during expansion.

### Cost Structure
- Web/mobile development, hosting, mapping services, and restroom-location verification.
- Initial mapping and ongoing updates for each campus or shopping mall.
- Venue onboarding, customer support, and advertiser outreach if advertising is tested.

### Revenue Streams
- **Venue licensing:** Annual fees from universities or shopping mall operators for a maintained restroom directory and an administration dashboard.
- **Advertising:** Monthly sponsored cards or banner placements purchased by nearby businesses, clearly separated from restroom recommendations.
- **Setup fees:** One-time fees for mapping and onboarding a venue, where that work is not included in its license.

**Assumptions to Validate**
- **Needs validation:** Students experience this problem often enough to use a dedicated app.
- **Needs validation:** Students are willing to share their location.
- **Needs validation:** Universities are willing to provide restroom data.
- **Needs validation:** Universities would pay for the service.
- **Needs validation:** Mall operators would pay for restroom navigation rather than rely only on their existing directories.
- **Needs validation:** The app can attract enough repeat local usage to sell advertising; ad income should not be assumed during the pilot.
- **Needs validation:** Venue fees can cover the recurring cost of keeping each location accurate.

## Lean Canvas 2 — Find the Correct Floor

### Problem
1. Students may know the correct building but not which floor contains a restroom.
2. Large campus buildings can be difficult to navigate.
3. General navigation apps usually stop at the building entrance.

### Solution
1. Show the floor number for every restroom.
2. Provide indoor floor maps where available.
3. Give simple indoor directions such as "2nd floor, past the elevators."

### Unique Value Proposition
Navigation that continues inside the building and takes students to the exact restroom floor.

### Unfair Advantage
A detailed database of restroom floors and indoor locations across campus buildings.

### Customer Segments
- **Initial users:** New students and visitors who reach a campus building but cannot find the restroom floor or hallway.
- **Initial paying customer to test:** University facilities or campus wayfinding teams.
- **Expansion:** Visitors in multilevel shopping malls; mall operators and property managers as potential buyers.

### Key Metrics
- Indoor restroom searches
- Route completion rate
- User-reported navigation accuracy
- Number of mapped buildings
- Venue pilot-to-paid conversion and mapping/update effort per building.

### Channels
- Approved QR codes at building entrances, elevators, and campus orientation materials.
- Direct pilot proposals to university wayfinding teams and mall operators.
- Embedding the directory in a venue website, digital directory, or branded app.

### Cost Structure
- Obtaining permission to use floor plans, mapping restroom locations, and checking indoor directions.
- Updating maps after renovations, testing routes, hosting, and technical support.
- Optional integration work for a venue-branded directory; indoor-positioning hardware is not assumed for the initial version.

### Revenue Streams
- **Indoor-directory licensing:** Annual fees from campuses and shopping malls for floor-level restroom maps.
- **Mapping and setup services:** One-time fees for preparing floor plans and initial location data.
- **White-label licensing:** A setup fee and ongoing license for using the maps inside a venue’s own branded website or app.

**Assumptions to Validate**
- **Needs validation:** Floor-level directions significantly reduce search time.
- **Needs validation:** Universities can provide floor plans.
- **Needs validation:** Indoor navigation can be accurate enough to be useful.
- **Needs validation:** Students prefer indoor directions over simply asking someone.
- **Needs validation:** A shopping mall would pay for this service despite already having a building directory.
- **Needs validation:** Floor labels, text directions, and QR-code starting points provide enough value before live indoor positioning is available.
- **Needs validation:** Each venue’s map-update workload is affordable within the proposed license.

## Lean Canvas 3 — Check Whether a Restroom Is Open

### Problem
1. Students may walk to a restroom and find it locked.
2. Building access changes at night, on weekends, and during holidays.
3. Static map information may not reflect current availability.

### Solution
1. Show building operating hours.
2. Mark restrooms as open, closed, or unverified.
3. Allow students to report temporary closures or locked doors.

### Unique Value Proposition
Know whether a restroom is available before spending time walking there.

### Unfair Advantage
Restroom-level availability data that combines official building schedules with recent student reports.

### Customer Segments
- **Initial users:** Students studying late in libraries, labs, or other campus buildings.
- **Initial paying customer to test:** University facilities or building-operations departments.
- **Expansion:** Mall shoppers and employees near opening or closing time; mall operations teams as potential buyers.

### Key Metrics
- Availability checks
- Accuracy of open/closed status
- Number of user reports
- Number of failed restroom trips avoided
- Paying venue pilots and staff time required to keep availability records current.

### Channels
- Late-night study communities, library information pages, and the bathroom finder.
- Pilot outreach to university building managers and shopping mall operations teams.
- Venue websites and staff-managed restroom status pages.

### Cost Structure
- Collecting building and restroom access hours, integrating approved schedules, and maintaining status records.
- User-report moderation, staff update tools, hosting, and support.
- Checking temporary closures and showing report timestamps rather than promising continuously verified availability.

### Revenue Streams
- **Venue status-management licensing:** Annual campus or mall fees for a directory with hours, temporary closures, and staff update tools.
- **Integration fees:** Optional setup charges to connect the venue’s operating schedules or existing systems.
- **Shared-platform advertising:** Clearly labeled local business advertisements outside urgent availability alerts, once usage is sufficient.

**Assumptions to Validate**
- **Needs validation:** Locked restrooms are a frequent enough problem.
- **Needs validation:** Official building-hours data is available and accurate.
- **Needs validation:** Students will submit real-time status updates.
- **Needs validation:** Community reports remain reliable.
- **Needs validation:** Venue staff would keep closure information current and find enough operational value to pay.
- **Needs validation:** Building opening hours reflect actual visitor restroom access; being open does not automatically mean every restroom is available.
- **Needs validation:** Advertising can support the wider app without distracting users who need an available restroom urgently.

- Percentage of listings with verified accessibility data
- Successful route completions
- Accessibility corrections submitted
- Paying venue pilots and the cost of keeping access attributes verified.

### Channels
- Campus accessibility offices and student groups, with users involved in testing the information and routes.

- **Needs validation:** Users trust crowdsourced accessibility information.
- **Needs validation:** Universities will help verify the data.
- **Needs validation:** Campus and mall operators have an identified budget owner willing to pay for accessible wayfinding.
- **Needs validation:** Users find the information reliable enough for their particular needs; payment cannot determine an accessibility label or verification status.
- **Needs validation:** Basic accessibility details and route options can remain free to users under the venue-funded model.

- **Initial paying customer to test:** University facilities or campus navigation teams.

- Route requests
- Predicted versus actual travel time
- Percentage of users choosing the fastest recommendation
- Average estimated time saved

## Lean Canvas 10 — Find a Restroom Along the Route to Class

### Problem
1. Students may need a restroom while already traveling to their next class.
2. The nearest restroom may require a large detour.
3. A restroom stop can make a student late to class.

### Solution
1. Let the student enter a destination or connect a class schedule.
2. Identify restrooms close to the existing route.
3. Show the extra walking time for each option.

### Unique Value Proposition
Find a restroom on the way to your next destination without taking a large detour.

### Unfair Advantage
Combining campus restroom data, indoor navigation, and student destinations in a single route-planning system.

### Customer Segments
- **Initial users:** Students walking between back-to-back classes who need a restroom without a large detour.
- **Initial paying customer to test:** University student-services or campus navigation teams.
- **Expansion:** Shopping mall visitors heading to a store, restaurant, or exit; mall operators as potential buyers.
- **Potential advertisers:** Businesses along a user-selected route, without using sensitive restroom preferences for targeting.

### Key Metrics
- Route-based restroom searches
- Average detour time
- Percentage of recommendations accepted
- Completed routes
- Repeat usage during class days
- Paid venue pilots and advertiser interest relative to route-search activity.

### Channels
- The bathroom finder, campus orientation, and student destination-entry tools.
- Pilot outreach to university navigation teams and shopping mall operators.
- Mall websites, QR-code maps, and optional calendar integrations after the simpler version is tested.

### Cost Structure
- Routing and map services, restroom data maintenance, development, and hosting.
- Venue onboarding and support; optional calendar or venue-directory integrations.
- Business sponsorship tools if advertising is tested after sufficient usage.

### Revenue Streams
- **Venue licensing:** Annual campus or shopping mall fees for restroom stops integrated into visitor navigation.
- **White-label licensing:** Setup and ongoing fees for embedding the feature in a venue’s own app or directory.
- **Sponsored business cards:** Clearly labeled paid offers from businesses near the user-selected route; sponsorship does not change the route or restroom recommendation.

**Assumptions to Validate**
- **Needs validation:** Students frequently need restroom stops while traveling between classes.
- **Needs validation:** Students will share destination or calendar information.
- **Needs validation:** Route-based recommendations provide enough extra value.
- **Needs validation:** Calendar and campus-map integrations are feasible.
- **Needs validation:** Mall visitors need restroom stops while heading to another destination often enough to use the feature.
- **Needs validation:** Venue operators would pay for the routing feature, and businesses would pay for clearly labeled placements at the actual audience size.
- **Needs validation:** Entering a destination manually provides enough value before users are asked to connect a calendar.
- **Needs validation:** Advertisements can be shown without introducing detours or distracting users trying to reach their destination.
