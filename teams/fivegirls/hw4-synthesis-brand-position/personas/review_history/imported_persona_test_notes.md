# Persona review notes 
## judge-maya feedback
- Likely positive: "Know before you go" and explicit cleanliness basics answer the walking-into-a-dirty-bathroom problem.
- Likely concern: "recent reports" is too vague unless the landing page shows a sample timestamp and specific conditions.
- Suggested revision: Show a sample result with “last checked”, “floor dry?”, “soap?”, and “stall usable?”; explicitly mark unknowns.
- Research basis: C02 P2 (cleanliness/usability, delayed travel, need for recent updates).

## judge-chloe feedback
- Likely positive: precise checkboxes and explicit unverified labels meet her preference for objective details.
- Likely concern: Not enough explanation of what makes a report trustworthy: last-check time, number of agreeing people, and report age.
- Suggested revision: Surface report time and agreement count right in the preview card; make an update a short checkbox flow.
- Research basis: C01 P3 (soap/paper/floor/stall, time, corroboration, rejection of long reviews).

## judge-alex feedback
- Likely positive: the positioning includes inside-the-building floor access and entry rules.
- Likely concern: "floor access" is ambiguous: does it actually disclose stairs, entrance-level restroom, and elevator dependence?
- Suggested revision: Name bathroom level and route from entrance in the hero demonstration; never equate building-entry access with restroom access.
- Research basis: A02 P1 (suitcase, downstairs restroom, missing interior information).

## What disagreement should teach the team
Maya and Chloe both demand recent evidence, but Maya's immediate decision is whether conditions are usable, whereas Chloe seeks corroboration and a granular checklist. Alex may reject a cleanliness-focused page even if it is excellent for those two: the navigation path and actual entry restrictions are his decisive criteria. This is evidence for distinct user segments rather than three interchangeable enthusiastic reviewers.
