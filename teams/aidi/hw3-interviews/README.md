## Overview

This repository contains the customer interview materials and findings developed for HW 3. The interviews were designed to evaluate two possible product directions for the International Student Application Assistant Platform:

- **Prompt A — Personalized Fit Based School List:** A tool that recommends personalized reach, target, and safety schools based on a student's academic background, intended major, budget, location preferences, and other individual factors.
- **Prompt B — School Profile Snapshot:** A tool that provides a concise and standardized overview of each university, including admissions difficulty, cost, academic strengths, location, deadlines, international student support, and key advantages and disadvantages.

The purpose of the interviews was to understand how international students currently research and select U.S. colleges, identify their main difficulties, and evaluate their reactions to the two proposed ideas.

## Interview Method

The research used semi-structured customer interviews conducted with the assistance of AI agents.

The intended participants were international students who were currently applying to U.S. undergraduate programs or had completed the application process within the previous three years. Prompt B participants were also expected to have researched multiple U.S. colleges.

Each interview followed these procedures:

1. The participant was assigned an anonymous participant code.
2. The participant selected English or Chinese as the interview language.
3. The interviewer explained the purpose of the student research and obtained consent before beginning.
4. Participants were informed that their responses would be shared with the AIDI student team and might be analyzed using AI.
5. Participants were instructed not to provide sensitive identifying information and were allowed to skip questions or stop the interview at any time.
6. The AI agent asked one question at a time and used follow-up questions when clarification was needed.
7. Chinese responses were preserved in their original language and accompanied by English translations.
8. Each completed interview was documented in a Word file and summarized in a Post Interview Snapshot.

The Snapshot analysis includes six Prompt A interviews and five Prompt B interviews. Prompt B participant B06 was not included in the Snapshot analysis because the source document did not contain a Post Interview Snapshot.

## Results

### Prompt A Results

The Prompt A interviews provided evidence that international students experience difficulty creating personalized and balanced college lists.

Common problems included:

- Difficulty determining whether a school should be classified as a reach, target, or safety option.
- Uncertainty about whether a university fits the student's individual academic background and preferences.
- Admissions information being scattered across official websites, rankings, social media, and informal sources.
- Difficulty evaluating personal fit beyond rankings and general admission statistics.
- Significant time spent researching and comparing schools.
- Unequal access to knowledgeable school counselors or admissions guidance.

Participants generally reacted positively to the idea of a personalized school-list tool. They were especially interested in recommendations that explain why a school might fit a student's profile rather than simply producing a list of universities.

The most common concerns were:

- Outdated or inaccurate admissions information.
- False precision in admission predictions.
- Algorithmic bias against students from particular countries or school backgrounds.
- Privacy concerns related to academic and financial information.
- Lack of transparency about recommendation logic and data sources.

Willingness to pay was mixed. Some participants expressed willingness to pay for an early version, while others wanted to review a sample or test the recommendation quality before making a purchase. Several participants no longer personally needed the product because they had already completed the application process.

### Prompt B Results

The Prompt B interviews provided evidence that international students find college research fragmented, repetitive, and difficult to standardize.

Common problems included:

- Important information being distributed across many university webpages and third-party platforms.
- Repeatedly searching for tuition, scholarships, application requirements, deadlines, academic programs, and location information.
- Difficulty determining whether third-party information is current and reliable.
- Using screenshots, personal notes, or spreadsheets to compare colleges.
- Needing to verify important information through official university websites.
- Difficulty obtaining concise information that is specifically relevant to international students.

Participants generally viewed the one-page school profile as useful for early-stage research and comparison. The most frequently requested information included:

- Tuition and living costs.
- Scholarships and financial aid.
- Application requirements and deadlines.
- Admissions difficulty.
- Academic and major strengths.
- Location and campus environment.
- International student support.
- Employment and career opportunities.
- Source links and update dates.

The largest concern was whether a short summary could remain accurate, current, complete, and unbiased. Participants consistently preferred information that could be verified through official sources.

Willingness to pay was again mixed. One participant expressed a clear willingness to pay $10, while others wanted to see a strong sample, suggested a lower price, or would only consider paying if the tool clearly saved time.

### Overall Findings

Both ideas received meaningful but not conclusive positive signals.

Across both sets of interviews, participants expressed a need for information that is:

- Centralized.
- Reliable and current.
- Transparent about its sources.
- Relevant to international applicants.
- Personalized to the student's circumstances.
- Easy to compare across universities.

Prompt A addresses the deeper decision problem of determining personal fit and building a balanced application list. Prompt B addresses the practical research problem of collecting and comparing reliable school information.

The findings suggest that the two concepts may complement each other. A standardized and well-sourced school profile could provide the information foundation for a personalized school-list recommendation system.

However, trust is essential for both ideas. Participants want visible source links, update dates, explanations of recommendation logic, and clear acknowledgment of uncertainty. The interviews support continued prototyping and testing but do not yet establish broad market demand or product-market fit.

## Limitations

This research has several limitations:

- The sample was small and was not randomly selected.
- The findings are based on participants' self-reported experiences.
- Some participants had already completed the application process and no longer had an immediate need for the proposed products.
- Some interview questions were skipped or did not receive detailed answers.
- One Prompt B participant completed the application process outside the intended three-year eligibility period, so that interview should be interpreted cautiously.
- Prompt B participant B06 was excluded from the Snapshot analysis because the source document did not include a Post Interview Snapshot.
- Most statements about willingness to pay were hypothetical and were not supported by actual purchases.

The findings should therefore be treated as directional evidence for further research and prototype testing.
