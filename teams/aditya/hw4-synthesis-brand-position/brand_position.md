# Brand Position: Gable

**Team Name:** Aditya
**Date:** October 9, 2026

**Purpose:** This document captures Gable's thesis, identity, and canonical language so that everything we build speaks with one voice: landing page, copy, features, pitch. It is written for agents as much as for us, and we update it as discovery continues.

**Source material:** our five housing interviews (Ruby, Emma, Tina, Marcus, Jasmine; HW3 snapshots and analysis).

---

## Brand Thesis

> **Finding a place shouldn't take thirty tours and a leap of faith.**

Students searching for housing in NYC lose weeks to two things: chasing agents and listers to set up viewings, and not knowing which people and listings are real. Gable takes on the back-and-forth for them and gives them honest information from other students, so they spend their time deciding, not coordinating and second-guessing.

---

## Positioning Statement

> **For students and recent grads looking for an apartment, sublet, or room in NYC, Gable is the housing search app that handles the back-and-forth of setting up viewings and shows you what other students say about a place and who's behind a listing. Tt coordinates with agents and listers for you and is built on real students' accounts, not marketing photos.**

---

## Brand Values

- **Your time is the product.** Every feature should remove a message, a phone call, or a wasted tour. If a feature adds steps for the renter, it needs a very good reason.

- **Renters first.** We work for the person searching, not for brokers or landlords. When their interests conflict, we side with the renter.

---

## Target Identity

> **Gable is made for a student or recent grad who has a semester start, internship, or lease date coming up. We do the heavy lifting for coordinating and trust so you don't have to.**

They're organized and willing to put in the work, and they already have. They've toured place after place and messaged dozens of people. What they're missing isn't listings. It's someone to handle the coordination and a way to tell what's real.

---

## Canonical Language

Phrases we use repeatedly, taken from what interviewees actually said:

- **"Skip the back-and-forth"** (Emma described scheduling with agents as extensive back-and-forth; this is the coordination promise)
- **"Not thirty tours"** (from Emma: "We visited, like, 30 places, and we messaged probably way more than that")
- **"Stop messaging twenty people"** (from Marcus: "I messaged probably like twenty people and half of them never answered")
- **"Know what's real before you go, and before you pay"** (from Marcus's "I wasn't even sure were real," Jasmine's worry about sending money first, and Emma's misleading photos)
- **"From students who've lived there"** (how we describe reviews; specific about the source)
- **"Somebody who knows somebody"** (from Jasmine: "I'd rather rent from somebody who knows somebody I know…"; use when describing school and mutual connections)

---

## Language to Avoid

- **Avoid:** "safe," "scam-proof," "guaranteed," "100% verified" — **Because:** we can verify that someone is a student and pass along what other students say; we can't guarantee a place or a deal. Overpromising is exactly what our users have learned to distrust, and it creates liability when something goes wrong.
- **Avoid:** "the best apartments," "hand-picked," "curated quality" — **Because:** we don't inspect apartments. Our trust promise is honest information, not a quality guarantee.
- **Avoid:** "AI-powered," "your AI leasing agent," "smart" — **Because:** users care that the scheduling gets done, not how. Describe the job ("we set up your viewings"), not the technology.
- **Avoid:** "every listing in one place," "the next StreetEasy," "all-in-one" — **Because:** we can't win on listing count against StreetEasy and Zillow, and promising coverage invites a comparison we'd lose.
- **Avoid:** "seamless," "revolutionize," "disrupt," "leverage" — **Because:** generic startup language. Our users talk about being ghosted, wasting weekends on tours, and getting scammed. We talk the way they do.

---

## Tone Guidelines

- **Relieving, not hyped.** Our users are tired. Sound like the friend who says "I'll handle it," not a launch announcement.
- **Plain, not salesy.** Short sentences and everyday words. The specific detail ("we'll find a time with the agent and put it on your calendar") over the abstract claim ("streamlined touring").
- **Reassuring, not alarmist.** Name the scam worry plainly, then say what we do about it. Don't sell with fear.
- **Peer, not landlord.** Sound like a classmate who's been through the search, not a property-management company.

## Objection Handling

| Objection | Our response |
|-----------|--------------|
| "No. I didn't look at a single other place." (Tina, who found her sublease through her cousin) | Then you don't need us this time. Gable is for when the network runs out: the internship in a new city, the year your friends aren't moving. Come back then. |
| Will agents actually respond to an app instead of a person? (our own open question) | We contact agents the way renters do now, by email, text, and booking links, just without you doing it. You confirm before anything is booked. |
| I'd stick with StreetEasy until a new app is trusted and popular. (Emma, paraphrased) | Keep finding listings wherever you like. Gable handles what StreetEasy leaves to you: setting up the viewings and checking what's real. |
| Will there be enough reviews or listings to be useful? (Marcus's concern about listings, paraphrased) | We start with one neighborhood and one campus so what's there is dense and current. A few honest reviews of the buildings you're actually considering beat thousands of reviews of buildings you're not. |
| I probably wouldn't pay just to browse. (Jasmine, paraphrased) | Browsing and reviews are free. We'll test separately whether people value the coordination enough to pay for it. |

## Open questions and next steps

- **Which promise leads?** Trust came up in 4 of 5 interviews; coordination came up in 1 (Emma) but at the highest intensity (9/10). Next week's landing page should test both: one headline on coordination, one on trust. Let signups decide which leads.
- **Agents are the other side of coordination.** We haven't talked to a single leasing agent or landlord. Whether they'll respond to coordinated requests is the biggest unknown in the product.
- **Reviews need volume.** Students move about once a year and rarely write reviews. We need a plan to seed reviews for specific buildings before reviews can carry trust. Check existing NYC apartment-review apps before building this part.
- **Listing source.** Coordination can work on top of listings from other sites, but sublets from students may need to be posted directly. This connects to the team's open question about aggregation vs. our own database and the legality of scraping.
