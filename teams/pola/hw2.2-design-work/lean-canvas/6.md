# Lean Startup Canvas — Predictive Campus Departure Assistant

## 1. Problem

University students with dense schedules often misjudge when they need to leave for their next class.

Existing calendar reminders usually assume that travel time is either fixed or irrelevant. They do not account for:

- The student's real-time location
- Exact classroom or room-level destination
- Indoor navigation within buildings
- Elevator and stair travel
- Building entrances and exits
- Walking speed
- Congestion
- Campus-specific shortcuts
- Differences between theoretical and realistic travel times

This creates three recurring problems:

1. **Minor lateness** — students arrive a few minutes late because they underestimate travel time.
2. **Unnecessary rushing** — students suddenly realize they need to leave and must rush across campus.
3. **Excessive safety margins** — students leave much earlier than necessary because they cannot reliably estimate travel time.

### Existing Alternatives

Students currently compensate through:

- Fixed calendar notifications
- Google Maps / Apple Maps
- Memorized walking times
- Checking the clock manually
- Leaving an arbitrary 5–15 minutes early
- Asking classmates about building locations
- Learning routes through repeated experience

These solutions require the student to manually connect **time + current location + destination + route conditions**.

### Primary Users

University students who:

- Have frequent back-to-back classes
- Move between multiple buildings
- Study on large campuses
- Attend classes in vertically complex buildings

- Have difficulty estimating indoor travel time

Particularly relevant at:

- Large urban universities
- Multi-campus universities
- Dense vertical campuses
- Universities embedded within cities

Students who:

- Professors moving between classrooms

- Researchers moving between buildings
- Campus visitors attending scheduled events

## 3. Unique Value Proposition

### Core Value Proposition

**Know exactly when to leave for your next class.**

Instead of reminding users that a class starts soon, the system tells them when they actually need to begin moving based on where they are and how long the real journey will take.

### High-Level Concept

**“Google Maps ETA + Calendar reminders, but for campus departure timing.”**

Or:

**A predictive alarm clock for physical movement between scheduled places.**

### Key Benefits

- Arrive on time without constantly checking the clock
- Reduce unnecessary rushing
- Avoid leaving excessively early
- Automatically adapt to changing location
- Learn personalized walking and routing behavior
- Navigate directly to the exact classroom rather than merely the building

---

## 4. Solution

A mobile and smartwatch application connected to:

- Calendar
- Real-time location
- High-resolution campus routing data
- TopoNavi or a similar indoor/outdoor routing engine

For each upcoming class, the system continuously calculates:

**Current Position → Exact Destination → Estimated Travel Time → Required Departure Time**

The system then generates a personalized **Leave-By Time**.

Example:

> CS Lecture  
> Starts: 2:40 PM  
> Current location: Butler Library, 4F  
> Destination: Mudd 833  
> Estimated trip: 11 min  
> Safety margin: 3 min  
>   
> **Leave by 2:26 PM**

### Notification Sequence

Example escalation:

- **T−10:** “You should leave in about 10 minutes.”
- **T−5:** stronger notification
- **Leave-by time:** smartwatch vibration
- **Late-departure threshold:** persistent / escalating alert

The system can stop escalating once it detects that the user is moving toward the destination.

### Adaptive Features

Over time, the system learns:

- User walking speed
- Preferred routes
- Typical elevator waiting time
- Route history
- Building transition time
- Personal safety margin
- Time required to pack up and leave
- Congestion patterns

The result becomes increasingly personalized.

## 8. Key Metrics

### North-Star Metric

**Percentage of predicted trips where the user arrives within the desired time window without leaving excessively early.**

For example:

> % of trips where arrival occurs between 0 and 5 minutes before class.

This captures the actual value proposition better than simply measuring app engagement.

### Supporting Metrics

**Activation**

- % of users connecting a calendar
- % adding their first class schedule
- % completing their first routed trip

**Engagement**

- Weekly routed trips per user
- Leave-by notifications acted upon
- Percentage of classes using predictive departure

**Accuracy**

- Predicted vs actual travel time
- Mean arrival-time error
- Percentage of ETA predictions within ±2 minutes

**Behavioral Impact**

- Reduction in reported lateness
- Reduction in excessive early arrival
- Reduction in rushed trips

**Retention**

- Week-1 retention
- Month-1 retention
- Semester retention

# Riskiest Assumptions

The product should test these assumptions before significant development.

### Assumption 1 — The problem is painful enough

Students may acknowledge being occasionally late but not consider it important enough to install another application.

**Test:** Interview students with back-to-back classes and quantify how often lateness or rushing occurs.

---

### Assumption 2 — Existing habits are insufficient

Students might already solve the problem adequately by memorizing campus travel times.

**Test:** Compare experienced students with first-year/new students.

---

### Assumption 3 — Students will grant location and calendar access

The product requires relatively sensitive permissions.

**Test:** Show a realistic onboarding prototype and measure willingness to connect both services.

---

### Assumption 4 — Higher routing precision materially improves departure predictions

Room-to-room routing may be technically impressive without creating enough additional user value over simple building-to-building estimates.

**Test:** Compare:

**Version A:** fixed building-level walking estimates  
vs.  
**Version B:** TopoNavi room-to-room estimates

Measure perceived usefulness and prediction accuracy.

---

### Assumption 5 — Users want active intervention

Persistent smartwatch vibration could be valuable for some users but annoying for others.

**Test:** Experiment with several alert intensities:

- Passive notification
- Escalating notification
- Haptic reminder
- Persistent haptic alert

---

# Recommended MVP

The first MVP does **not** need full indoor navigation or sophisticated machine learning.

Build:

1. Calendar integration
2. Current-location detection
3. Database of campus buildings
4. Building-to-building routing
5. User-defined safety margin
6. Dynamic leave-by calculation
7. Push notification at the calculated departure time

The core experiment is:

> **Will students repeatedly use a system that automatically tells them when to leave?**

Only after demonstrating demand should the product progressively add:

**building-level routing → entrance-aware routing → room-level routing → elevators → congestion → personalized travel-time prediction.**

---

# Core Lean Hypothesis

> For university students with dense, location-dependent schedules, automatically calculating a real-time personalized departure time will reduce lateness and unnecessary waiting enough that students repeatedly rely on the system throughout the semester.

The strongest validation signal would not simply be downloads or notification clicks.

It would be students beginning to **trust the Leave-By Time enough that they stop manually calculating when they need to leave.**
